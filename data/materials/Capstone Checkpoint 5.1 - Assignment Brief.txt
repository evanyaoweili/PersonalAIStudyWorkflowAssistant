Learning outcome addressed
4. Refine a capstone agent concept by specifying agent roles, coordination strategy, and
   communication approach for a multi-agent workflow.

At this stage, you will refine your capstone agent concept by designing a multi-agent
architecture that aligns with your selected problem. This checkpoint focuses on making
deliberate design decisions about agent roles, coordination strategy, and communication
approach, rather than implementation. The goal is to demonstrate architectural judgment by
clearly defining how multiple agents will collaborate to solve your problem and why a
multi-agent approach is appropriate.

Directions

You will develop a structured architecture plan that outlines how your system will be
organized as a multi-agent workflow. This includes defining the number of agents, assigning
roles and responsibilities, and specifying how agents will interact through coordination and
communication. Your design should reflect a clear understanding of trade-offs, including how
coordination decisions impact system reliability, complexity, and performance.

In your submission, address the following questions:
- What problem is your capstone agent designed to solve, and why does it require a
  multi-agent approach rather than a single agent? (For instance, a research-heavy task may
  benefit from a planner agent, a researcher agent, and a writer agent working together.)
- How many agents will your system include, and how did you determine the appropriate
  number? Consider task complexity, role separation, and diminishing returns from adding more
  agents.
- What are the defined roles and responsibilities of each agent?
- What coordination strategy will govern how agents interact (for example, sequential,
  iterative, graph-based, or hybrid)? Sequential workflows resemble assembly lines, while
  graph-based workflows resemble project networks with dependencies.
- How will agents communicate and exchange information throughout the workflow? One-way
  communication for efficiency, two-way for validation, or brainstorming for exploring
  multiple paths.
- What trade-offs did you consider in your design, particularly in terms of reliability,
  complexity, and coordination overhead? For example, adding feedback loops may improve
  reliability but increase latency.
- How does your proposed architecture support effective and scalable problem solving?

Aim for one to two pages, focusing on clarity and rationale rather than implementation
details. Include:
1. A clear description of your multi-agent system design
2. Defined agent roles and responsibilities
3. A description of the coordination and communication strategy
4. A rationale for your design decisions based on module concepts
